# Topic 3 Phase D: design evidence for Play and Create

This document traces the Topic 3 (Differential Calculus in ℝⁿ) Play and Create activities to published research. It is for internal documentation, the project report and the lecturer. **None of it is shown in the learner interface.** The same information is held in code in `content/topics/differential/evidence.ts` (`RN_ACTIVITY_EVIDENCE`, attached to each activity as `evidenceBasis`).

> The cited literature supports the design principle; it does not establish the effectiveness of this specific MathArt Connect activity.

MathArt Connect is designed to support Calculus learning. Its games and art have not been evaluated, and no claim is made that they improve learning or performance.

## Design principles

- **P1. The mathematics is the mechanic** (Thomas 2025). Every scored action is a decision about a domain, a partial derivative, a differential or a chain-rule path. Typed answers are checked at several points by the same engine as My Working (`lib/math/partial.ts`). Feedback explains every choice. Random tapping is not rewarded: no option is correct in two rounds of the same game, and the position of the correct option varies (tested in `__tests__/rn-phase-d.test.tsx`).
- **P2. Dynamic, manipulable visualisation linked to the symbols** (Schönherr 2024). Studios redraw surfaces, slices, trees and grids as the learner changes a course-aligned setting, with "Math behind my art" showing the matching formulas.
- **P3. Art built from mathematical structure** (Sylviani 2024). The shapes are generated by the mathematics; the learner completes a project by explaining that mathematics in their own words. COURSE-ALIGNED settings are visibly separated from CREATIVE EXTENSION styling, which never changes the mathematics (tested).

Every studio artwork is a stack of up to five layers. Each layer has its own COURSE-ALIGNED settings (the mathematics) and CREATIVE EXTENSION settings (rotated copies, colour roles, line weight, opacity, the surface under slices), and can be shown or hidden, reordered, duplicated, renamed or deleted. Starters are layered compositions, for example slices with y held constant over slices with x held constant, or the paths to s and to t lit in two colours.

The view (zoom, move, Fit) only frames the artwork; it uses sliders and buttons rather than pinch gestures, so page scrolling on a phone is never captured. The optional animation shows mathematics in motion: the level value rising through the bands (Domain Region), the held value sweeping the slices across the surface (Surface Slice), a pulse travelling down each lit chain-rule path (Chain Network), and one curve of constant coordinate sweeping through the grid (Coordinate Grid). It does not run while the device asks to reduce motion. "Math behind my art" explains every visible layer and states that copies and opacity change only the look. Projects saved before layers existed reopen as one layer.

Scope note: tangent planes and estimating Δz with dz belong to Topic 4 and are not used anywhere in Topic 3 (§3.3 defines the total differential only).

## Reflect and Review

- **Reflect:** one mathematical reflection question per subtopic (`content/topics/differential/review.ts`), saved to the learner's history with a confidence rating and a named difficulty. Never graded.
- **Review:** key concepts, formulas, conditions and common errors from Chapter 3; one App practice retrieval question per subtopic (written for the app, labelled, and kept out of the locked practice counts); then the learner's real history, open mistakes and one next action. No mastery percentages. Clairaut's conditions are referred to, not restated, so the printed Theorem 3.2.1 is never silently corrected (MR3-02).
