# Topic 2 Phase D: design evidence for Play and Create

This document traces the Topic 2 (Area and Definite Integrals) Play and Create activities to published research. It is for internal documentation, the project report and the lecturer. **None of it is shown in the learner interface.** The same information is held in code in `content/topics/area/evidence.ts` (`ACTIVITY_EVIDENCE`, attached to each activity as `evidenceBasis`).

> The cited literature supports the design principle; it does not establish the effectiveness of this specific MathArt Connect activity.

MathArt Connect is designed to support Calculus learning. Its games and art have not been evaluated, and no claim is made that they improve learning or performance. The design is informed by prior research on game-based calculus learning, mathematical visualisation and visual-art integration.

## Design principles drawn from the sources

- **P1. The mathematics is the mechanic** (Thomas 2025). Every scored action in a game is a calculus decision: choose a rule, set n, place x, set a height, choose a card, combine integrals, compare functions. No points are attached to a quiz wrapper. Feedback explains why. Random tapping is not rewarded:
  - Prediction rounds alternate their correct answer.
  - Tolerance rounds limit n, so maximising n with an endpoint rule fails.
  - A comparison that proves nothing is shown as "nothing follows" and does not finish the round.
- **P2. Dynamic, manipulable visualisation linked to the symbols** (Schönherr 2024). Every round and studio redraws the graph as the learner changes a value, and shows the matching expression and value.
- **P3. Riemann sums made visible and dynamic** (Tatar & Zengin 2016). Rectangles for left, right, midpoint and sample points change with n and are seen to approach the integral.
- **P4. Art built from mathematical structure** (Sylviani 2024). Learners create art whose shapes are generated by the mathematics. They explain that mathematics in their own words, for interest and creative investigation. Course-aligned settings are visibly separated from "Creative extension" styling.

Every studio shows a "Math behind my art" readout for each visible layer: the function, [a, b], n, Δx, the rule, the Riemann-sum expression and its value, the signed area and an explanation. For accumulation layers it shows A(x) and A′(x) = f(x); for trails, A(t) and its limit. The learner completes a project by explaining the mathematics in their own words.

### Reflect and Review (D3, D4)

These are not game or art mechanics and are not tied to the four sources above.

- **Reflect** asks one mathematical question per subtopic. The answer is saved to the learner's history as text.
- **Review** contains:
  - the recap from the notes: key concepts, formulas, theorem conditions and common errors;
  - one or two **App practice** retrieval questions per subtopic, written for the app and labelled as such;
  - the learner's real history and a next action.

No mastery is claimed.

## Where this is enforced in code

- `content/topics/area/evidence.ts`: sources, principles, `ACTIVITY_EVIDENCE` and `EVIDENCE_LIMITATION` (internal).
- `content/topics/area/activities.ts`: each game has `evidenceBasis` and `alignment: 'course'`; each studio has `evidenceBasis` and `alignment: 'creative'`.
- `__tests__/area-games.test.ts`: checks that every game round is solvable by a correct decision, that wrong decisions are rejected with an explanation, that fixed tapping cannot win, and that no source names appear in learner-facing tutorials.
- `__tests__/area-art.test.ts`: checks that templates are distinct and generated from the mathematics, that "Math behind my art" values are correct, and that creative settings never change the mathematics.
