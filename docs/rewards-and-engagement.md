# Rewards, weekly goals and ambient art: design and research basis

The reward system is informed by gamification research and established educational-app patterns; its usefulness within MathArt Connect remains to be evaluated. Nothing here claims that these specific rewards improve learning, motivation or results.

## What was built

| Part                | Where                                        | Summary                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Badges (22)         | `features/rewards/achievements.ts`           | Derived from the learner's real record. Firsts, practice, lessons, study days, XP, and four Topic 4 badges                                                      |
| Weekly goals        | `features/rewards/weeklyGoals.ts`            | Optional; Monday to Sunday in study days (UTC+2); 3 practice sessions, 2 mistakes put right, 2 Explore tools, 1 reflection; last week shown only for comparison |
| Personal bests      | Rewards screen (`app/(app)/rewards.tsx`)     | Current and longest streak, XP, badges, next badge ("15 XP to go"). No leaderboard and no comparison with other learners                                        |
| Milestone moments   | `features/rewards/CelebrationBanner.tsx`     | One small card on Home for a new badge, a completed topic or completed weekly goals; shown once per device; fades in (instant under Reduce Motion); no confetti |
| Home summary        | `features/rewards/RewardsSummaryCard.tsx`    | Weekly goal progress and the next badge, under the learning snapshot and smaller than Continue Learning                                                         |
| Cosmetic unlocks    | `features/rewards/rewardsStore.ts`           | Four Home backdrops (curves, Lissajous figure, contour lines, vector field) unlocked by badge count. No learning content is locked                              |
| Badge notifications | `features/notifications/useNotifications.ts` | One in-app notification per dated badge, stable id `badge-<id>` (never duplicated)                                                                              |
| Ambient art         | `components/art/AmbientMathArt.tsx`          | Sign in and registration (all auth screens), onboarding, the top of Home, two empty states. Never behind lessons, formulas, practice or My Working              |

### Badge criteria (completion rules)

| Badge                              | Earned when the record shows                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| First Practice                     | a practice answer checked in My Working                                                           |
| First Exploration                  | an Explore tool completed (the tool's own completion rule, not opening it)                        |
| First Creation                     | a Create project saved                                                                            |
| First Reflection                   | a reflection saved                                                                                |
| First Game                         | every round of a game completed                                                                   |
| 5 / 10 / 25 Correct Answers        | that many different practice questions answered correctly (repeats of a question count once)      |
| Mistakes Put Right                 | 3 answers that were wrong at first, answered correctly again (resolved review items)              |
| Subtopic Complete / Topic Complete | every required lesson step of a subtopic / every subtopic of a topic                              |
| 3-, 7-, 14-Day Streak              | the longest run of consecutive study days (profiles.longest_streak)                               |
| 100 / 250 / 500 XP                 | the XP total                                                                                      |
| Gradient Explorer (Topic 4)        | Gradient Probe completed and 3 different gradient questions correct                               |
| Plane Builder (Topic 4)            | Plane Builder game completed and 2 different tangent-plane questions correct                      |
| Critical Point Detective (Topic 4) | critical points found correctly in 3 different questions                                          |
| Classification Expert (Topic 4)    | every critical point found and classified correctly in 5 different Second Partials Test questions |

## Integrity rules

- **No reward for tapping or opening pages.** Every badge and goal counts saved learning records only.
- **No duplicate rewards.** Badges are a function of the record, so each is earned exactly once and its date never moves. Badges carry no XP; the XP rules in `record_activity` are unchanged (once-per-day awards and idempotent activity ids, see `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`).
- **Persistence.** Badges and goals are rebuilt from the synced record, so they survive reopening and reinstalling. Only device choices (milestones already celebrated, weekly goals on or off, chosen backdrop) are kept on the device, as for notification read state. On first use, milestones already reached are recorded quietly, so a long-standing learner is not shown a flood of moments.
- **No migration.** The schema's `achievements` / `user_achievements` tables are written server-side only (RLS); deriving badges needs no new table, column or policy, and RLS is unchanged.
- **Language.** Encouraging and factual ("1 more practice session to complete this week's practice sessions goal"); a missed goal is never shown as a failure.

## Ambient art

Each variant is real mathematics drawn once in the existing palette at low opacity: y = sin x + 0.3 sin 3x and y = cos 0.8x·e^(−0.05x); the Lissajous curve x = sin 3t, y = sin 2t; level curves of a two-peak surface; the gradient field of x² − y². Motion is a slow transform of the finished drawing (20–40 s per cycle, native driver): nothing is redrawn per frame. It ignores touch (`pointerEvents="none"`), is hidden from screen readers, stays still under Reduce Motion, and pauses when its screen is not focused or the app is in the background.

## Research basis

Interaction patterns were compared with established learning apps (Duolingo: daily streaks, XP, short goals; Brilliant: interactive, visual problem solving with immediate feedback). No visual design, wording or branded asset was copied.

| Pattern                             | Source                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | What it supports here                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Gamification of learning overall    | Sailer, M., & Homner, L. (2020). The gamification of learning: A meta-analysis. _Educational Psychology Review, 32_(1), 77–112. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Small positive effects on cognitive, motivational and behavioural outcomes on average; effects vary with design, so elements are tied to learning behaviour                                              |
| Mixed and context-dependent results | Hamari, J., Koivisto, J., & Sarsa, H. (2014). Does gamification work? A literature review of empirical studies on gamification. _Proceedings of HICSS 2014_, 3025–3034. https://doi.org/10.1109/HICSS.2014.377                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Gamification is not uniformly effective, hence restraint and evaluation                                                                                                                                  |
| Badges in a university course       | Hakulinen, L., Auvinen, T., & Korhonen, A. (2015). The effect of achievement badges on students' behavior: An empirical study in a university-level computer science course. _International Journal of Emerging Technologies in Learning, 10_(1), 18–29.                                                                                                                                                               | Badges can change study behaviour without affecting grades: badges here are milestones, not grades                                                                                                       |

| Goals                               | Locke, E. A., & Latham, G. P. (2002). Building a practically useful theory of goal setting and task motivation. _American Psychologist, 57_(9), 705–717. https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Specific, attainable goals with progress feedback: weekly goals with visible progress                                                                                                                    |

| Practice testing and feedback       | Dunlosky, J., et al. (2013). Improving students' learning with effective learning techniques. _Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14_(1), 4–58. https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266 ; Hattie, J., & Timperley, H. Pushing the limits of game-based instruction in calculus. _International Journal of Game-Based Learning, 15_(1), 1–23. https://doi.org/10.4018/IJGBL.385391 ; Molnar, A. (2019). The effect of gamification elements on engagement and achievement in Calculus 1 and 2. _IAFOR International Conference on Education, Hawaii 2019_ (conference paper) | Game-based calculus instruction (already the basis of the app's games); a calculus course with badges, points and progress bars reported higher engagement but samples too small for achievement effects |
| Visual, interactive learning        | Schönherr, J., Strohmaier, A. R., & Schukajlow, S. (2024). Learning with visualizations helps: A meta-analysis of visualization interventions in mathematics education. _Educational Research Review, 45_, 100639. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2024.100639                                                                                                                                                        | Ambient art is decoration only; the learning visualisations stay in Explore                                                                                                                              |

**Limitations.** No peer-reviewed study of badges or weekly goals specifically in university calculus was found; the calculus evidence above is game-based instruction and one conference paper. None of the sources evaluated MathArt Connect. The reward system's usefulness within MathArt Connect remains to be evaluated.
